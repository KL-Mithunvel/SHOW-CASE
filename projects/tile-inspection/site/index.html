A machine that looks at, listens to and sorts every clay tile.

A camera measures cracks and broken corners, a sprung striker taps each tile and listens for hidden cracks, a decision tree on a Raspberry Pi combines both into one grade, and a Cartesian gantry places the tile in its bin. When the evidence is missing or unclear, the tile goes to a person instead of getting a guessed grade.

01 · The problem

Nine people sort 20,160 tiles a day by hand.

SMTW grades fired clay roofing tiles by eye and by tapping them. It works, but it is tiring, nobody records why a tile was downgraded, and the defect never gets traced back to a kiln batch.

₹0.575manual sorting cost per tile (₹11,583 a day)
2.17 %of 53,040 tiles were broken
18.6 %fell into the two lowest grades (Grade 4 + 5)
0defect causes recorded on the daily sorting sheets

Breakage climbs with tile size, so thresholds must be set per size, and the case for automation is strongest exactly where the manual line loses most: the large tiles.

02 · The line

Four stations, one tile at a time, every decision logged.

Each station is its own computer. It processes its own raw data and publishes only a compact result over MQTT on an isolated network, so the line keeps working with no master PC attached.

Station 1 · CameraSeesFinds the tile on the belt, measures cracks, broken corners and squareness. Station 2 · AcousticListensA sprung striker taps the tile; the ring is checked for hidden cracks. Station 3 · DecisionDecidesA decision tree on the Raspberry Pi 5 turns camera and sound readings into one grade, or Hold when in doubt. Station 4 · Pick & placeSortsA Cartesian gantry with a vacuum cup places the tile in its grade bin.

04 · Stations

Following one tile down the belt.

STN 1

Camera station: measure, don't just label

The belt is found by its own colour every frame and everything outside it is blanked. The tile is segmented, tracked with its own id, and graded once, on the sharpest frame within ±6 % of a trigger line that the software learns from the belt's own axis.

Cracks and chips are measured in inches from the tile's known 9 in side, so no calibration target is needed and a threshold can be moved with evidence.

Corner check — any one flags the tile
ρ = Acontour / Arect < 0.86  ·  e = dmax / s > 0.22  ·  a = max(w,h)/min(w,h) > 1.15
Whole pipeline: 24 ms per 1080p frame (≈ 41 fps); grading one tile: 1.7 ms. Development laptop.
Tested on real belt footage · 5 videos · 92 tiles
STN 2

Acoustic station: the same tap, every time

A time-of-flight sensor sees the tile arrive. One sprung push-pull solenoid with a mallet head, on a 20 × 40 aluminium extrusion, is held up while energised and released onto the tile by gravity and its spring, so every tile is struck from the same height at the same point.

The clip is captured with a pre-trigger buffer, Hann-windowed and transformed. An intact tile should ring higher and longer; a cracked one lower and shorter.

Features per tap
fp = argmaxf ≥ 20 Hz |X(f)|  ·  spectral centroid · SNR · T20 from E(t) = ∫t∞ p²(τ) dτ
Separate desktop suite, Acoustic-Analysis: guided collection, noise removal, video slicing, labelled export. 237 tests.
Rig run on tiles · 28 real strikes recorded · good vs cracked not yet separable (see Models)
STN 3

Decision tree: camera + sound → one grade

The camera's readings (crack, corner damage, size) and the sound's readings (peak frequency and how loud or quiet the ring is) each become a grade. Both grades are fed into a rule-based decision tree on the Raspberry Pi 5, which picks the tile's final grade and bin. The full tree is drawn below.

The tree is fail-closed: a vote can lower a grade and can never rescue one, and any missing or doubtful input sends the tile to Hold for a person.

At the last branch
Gfinal = worse(Gcamera, Gsound)
Tiles are bound to taps by order, not time, because belt speed is open-loop. Every branch is an explicit rule with its own test.

STN 4

Pick & place: a gantry and its digital twin

A three-axis Cartesian gantry (the Y beam is two motors, cloned in hardware) with a vacuum cup. The Arduino UNO Q's Linux side owns the coordinate model, sequencer and operator screen; its microcontroller runs minimal firmware. A line-based ASCII protocol with checksums joins the two.

A Three.js digital twin and a simulated microcontroller let the sequencer and job runner be tested before the axes could move. The queue is first-in-first-out, de-duplicating, and applies back-pressure by not acknowledging when full.

Hardware 4 × NEMA 17 on TMC2208 drivers, CNC Shield V3.10 · AS5600 encoders on a TCA9548A mux · VL53L1X ToF · Mean Well LRS-150-12 · ≈ 22 W from the 12 V rail.
Firmware flashed and answering · axes moved under supervised homing · first pick next
05 · Operator and engineering software

Every node has a screen, and the master explains its own alarms.

The master PC is optional: it watches the line, keeps its own copy of every tile, and can send soft controls. Rule-based detectors find anomalies; a small local language model (llama3.2:3b, so no data leaves the control network) explains them in plain words. On synthetic data with seven planted faults, all seven were found with no false alarms.

Why hairline cracks disappear

The belt moves 435 px/s and the webcam's automatic exposure is 1/64 s, so each tile smears by about 7 px. A hairline crack is 1–3 px wide.

b = v · texp = 435 × 1/64 ≈ 6.8 px
Hairline crack
Motion smear

A faster shutter needs about 8× more light, and the webcam driver rejects gain changes. The fix is lighting or a slower belt, not software. Picking the sharpest frame near the line already made the graded frame 1.30× sharper.

07 · Camera and sound models

What the learned models can and cannot do yet.

Alongside the measured rules, we trained image classifiers for SMTW's own grades and analysed real tap recordings from the striker rig. Both are kept out of the final grade until they earn their place on the line's own data.

Six models, one validation split

YOLO26 classifiers and a fine-tuned ViT-base were trained to predict SMTW grades 3A, 3B, 4 and 5 from tile photos, then exported to ONNX and measured with the exact runtime that would deploy on the Raspberry Pi 5.

  • ViT-base is the most accurate at 92.1 %, but 343.5 MB and 110 ms a tile; INT8 cut that to 48.7 ms with no accuracy loss.
  • YOLO26n is 6.2 MB and 1.59 ms a tile at 82.9 %, the realistic choice for the Pi.
  • INT8 broke the small YOLO models: 12× slower and far less accurate. YOLO26s INT8 stopped predicting grade 3B at all.
  • Grade 4 is the hardest class for every model; most errors are 3A tiles called 4.

On the station's own webcam the model's confidence is about 30 %, below the 0.6 voting threshold, so it abstains until it is retrained on station-camera tiles.

28 real strikes, 22 labelled

Station sessions of 19 October 2026: room noise, the striker alone (25 strikes), and tiles through the full workflow with a USB microphone at 48 kHz. 22 strikes were labelled by hand: 17 good, 5 cracked.

  • The ring is real. A tile strike sits 10–15 dB above the striker alone from about 1 to 3 kHz, and up to about 20 dB above it between 4 and 8 kHz.
  • It fades fast. Energy falls about 30 dB in the first 100 ms; after about 200 ms tile and striker are within a few dB.
  • Good and cracked could not yet be told apart. Cracked tiles were 1–2.5 dB quieter in the low and mid bands with a slightly higher centroid, but no difference is statistically reliable (smallest permutation p = 0.11) with only 5 cracked tiles.

So the sound vote stays off. Next: more labelled cracked tiles, a calibrated microphone and a repeatable striker height.

08 · Faults found and fixed

What broke, and how we found out.

The report keeps every failed attempt in the text. A few of the thirteen:

Tracking

12 crossings for 7 tiles

Two tiles in frame swapped identity. Fix: track every region with nearest-centre matching and one crossing detector each → 7 of 7.

Crack check

98–100 % false positives

The tile's own edge read as a crack, found by calibrating on 311 intact photos. Fix: blank a border band first → 2–11 %.

Corner check

Diagonal chips slipped through

A chip halfway across only dropped ρ to 0.87. Fix: a second check on gap depth from a distance transform.

Model export

8 accuracy points lost, no error

OpenCV's resize filter differs from Pillow's antialiased bilinear. Fix: match preprocessing; deployed path verified byte-identical.

Bench bring-up

I²C mux not found on the UNO Q

A4/A5 belong to the Wire2 bus, not Wire. Fix: move firmware to Wire2 → mux and encoders found.

Watchdog

Gantry stopped on long moves

No command for 500 ms tripped LINK_TIMEOUT. Fix: the Linux side sends PING every 0.2 s during long moves.
